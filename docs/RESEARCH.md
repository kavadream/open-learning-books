# Research starting points

These are starting references, not proof that this product improves learning. Preserve scope, distinguish published studies from working papers, and check the relevant passage before making new claims.

- [How People Learn II](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/2): prior knowledge, culture, feedback, and transfer.
- [Dunlosky et al. learning-techniques review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266): practice testing and distributed practice.
- [Gentner et al. analogical encoding](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2003/learning-and-transfer-a-general-role-for-analogical-encoding): comparing negotiation cases and transfer.
- [Kestin et al. structured AI tutoring study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6): a specific university physics learning context; do not generalize directly to human-relations books.
- [Google pedagogical framework](https://research.google/pubs/googles-pedagogical-framework-for-generative-ai-to-support-human-learning/): active learning, cognitive load, motivation, adaptation, and metacognition.
- [H5P Interactive Book](https://h5p.com/content-types/interactive-book): an existing approach to structured interactive reading.
- [LearnHouse](https://github.com/learnhouse/learnhouse): an existing open learning-platform project.
- [Mindsmith AI Conversations](https://help.mindsmith.ai/en/articles/14095635-ai-conversations): an existing configurable voice and text practice product.
- [GitHub community-building guide](https://github.com/github/opensource.guide/blob/main/_articles/building-community.md): documentation, contribution entry points, and maintainership.
